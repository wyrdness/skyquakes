## Overview

Skyquakes are a class of unexplained acoustic phenomena reported across all inhabited continents and documented for centuries. The sounds are typically described as resembling distant cannon fire, sonic booms, rolling thunder, or heavy explosions, and are often heard during otherwise calm weather. They are reported worldwide under many regional names — mistpouffers (North Sea coasts), Barisal Guns (Ganges Delta), uminari ('cries from the sea', Japan), Seneca Guns (Seneca Lake and the Carolinas), bombes de mer (France), brontidi (Italy), cielomoto (Spanish-speaking world), and dentuman (Indonesia). Mechanisms proposed include distant thunder ducted through the atmosphere, meteor sonic booms, shallow earthquakes, methane releases from sea or lake floors, undersea cave collapses, breaking-wave acoustics, and military or industrial activity. The 1804 Lewis and Clark expedition recorded skyquake-like sounds near Great Falls, Montana. James Fenimore Cooper's 1850 short story 'The Lake Gun' helped popularise the Seneca Lake terminology. The U.S. Geological Survey has investigated the 'Seneca guns' along the Carolina coasts.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Atmospheric ducting of distant thunder or sonic booms** _(Atmospheric scientists; cited on the English Wikipedia article)_

Sound from distant thunderstorms or aircraft sonic booms can be 'ducted' through stable atmospheric layers and reach the surface tens or hundreds of kilometres away, with no visible source.

**Meteor sonic booms** _(Atmospheric and planetary scientists)_

Bolide entry events produce loud sonic booms that can be heard at the surface even when the meteor itself is unseen, particularly in daylight.

**Shallow earthquakes** _(U.S. Geological Survey)_

Very shallow micro-earthquakes can produce sounds at the surface without strong perceptible shaking; some Carolina-coast 'Seneca Guns' events are correlated with seismograph signals.

**Methane / biogas releases** _(Various atmospheric/earth scientists)_

Sudden releases of methane or other gases from sea-floor or lake-bed sediments may produce loud acoustic events.

**Underwater cave collapse and breaking waves** _(Various)_

Collapses of submarine caves or large breaking waves on rocky shores have been proposed as local sources of cannon-like reports.

### Skeptical

**Mundane source aggregation** _(Skeptical scientific commentary (general))_

Most skyquake reports are best explained by combinations of distant military activity, aircraft, sonic booms, thunder, blasting, or quarrying — even when these are not obvious to witnesses.
